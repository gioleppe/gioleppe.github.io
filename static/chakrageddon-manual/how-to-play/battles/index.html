Battles

Nobody at this festival fights with fists any more. Too much effort. Conflicts are settled on the astral plane, one Astral against one Astral, taking turns.

Battles start in two ways:

  • Wild Astrals drift out of encounter tiles: clouds of Nag Champa incense, dark bush, packed dancefloors, and water once you can float on it. A wild fight comes every few steps on those tiles, never right on top of the last one.
  • Trainers watch in a straight line. Walk into their line of sight and they shout "!", walk up to you, say their piece, and fight. You cannot run from a trainer: "There is no walking away from eye contact."

You carry up to six Astrals. The first one standing in your party goes out first.

Serotonin and Hydration​

Every Astral has two bars.

  • Serotonin is its health. Hits drain it. At zero the Astral fades out and cannot fight again until it is revived or rested.
  • Hydration is water for its moves. Every move costs water, shown as "Water" next to the move. Strong moves are thirsty. When an Astral cannot afford any of its moves, it can only Dry Heave: a weak, free hit that hurts itself too.

Keeping your team watered matters as much as keeping it healthy. Water Bottles and Electrolyte Sachets refill Hydration, and a Chai Tent fills everything. See Items and Shops.

The battle menu​

CommandWhat it does
FIGHTUse one of your Astral's moves (up to four).
BAGThrow a bagging item at a wild Astral, or use a recovery item on one of your party.
ASTRALSSwitch to another Astral. That uses up your turn.
RUNTry to get away. Wild battles only.

Switching, bagging and running always happen before anybody's move that turn.

Running does not always work, but every failed try makes the next one more likely, and it is easier if your Astral is at least as fast as the wild one.

Picking a move​

The move panel shows, for the move under the cursor:

  • its type,
  • its power ("Pow"), or "Status" for a move that does not deal damage,
  • its water cost, in red if your Astral cannot afford it,
  • and, once you have seen the foe before, a hint: resonates (it will hit hard) or weak (it will barely register).

For the full story of a move (accuracy, effects and odds, in plain words), look it up on the Astral's summary in the Fanny Pack.

Who goes first​

Some moves always go first (Jawling's Unsolicited Life Story is one). Otherwise the Astral with the higher BPM (speed) moves first. A tie is a coin flip.

Hitting, missing and critical hits​

  • Moves can miss. The game says it "drifts wide". Moves that only affect the user never miss.
  • A move that matches one of the user's own types hits 1.5 times as hard.
  • Type matchups can double or halve the damage, or more (see Astrals and Types). "It really resonates!" means it was super effective; "It barely registers..." means it was not.
  • Critical hits do 1.5 times the damage. They get more common the higher the battle is: from about one hit in sixteen when everyone is sober to one in four at the very top. That goes for both sides.

Status conditions​

An Astral can have one status at a time. A Thought Loop is the exception: it can come on top of another status. The battle screen shows a short tag next to the Astral's name.

StatusTagWhat it does
CottonmouthDRYIts physical moves hit half as hard, and it loses 3 Hydration at the end of every turn.
K-holeK-HOLEFor 2 to 4 turns its BPM drops to a quarter, and half the time it cannot move at all ("has forgotten what limbs are").
Thought LoopLOOPFor 2 to 5 turns, about a third of the time it either repeats its last move or hits itself in confusion.
Ego DeathEGOIt does nothing for 3 turns, then comes back with its Groundedness up 2 stages.
Jaw LockJAWIt loses Serotonin at the end of every turn, a little more each time, until it is cured.

How to get rid of them:

  • Tiger Balm cures any status, Thought Loops included. An Electrolyte Sachet cures Cottonmouth.
  • Switching out ends a Thought Loop.
  • A status ends when the Astral fades out, and every status is cleared by a rest at a Chai Tent.
  • Statuses stay after the battle until something cures them. Check your party.
  • The Clear Head ability, and the Sand Mala held item, stop Thought Loops and K-holes from happening at all. Earplugs in your bag make your Thought Loops a turn shorter.

Stat changes​

Some moves raise or lower stats for the rest of the fight: Vibe Attack, Ego Defense, Fractal power, Groundedness and BPM. "Rose!" is one stage, "rose sharply!" two. A stat can go up to six stages up or down. Each stage up adds half again, so two stages doubles a stat.

Stat changes last until the Astral leaves the field. Switching out resets them, the good ones as well as the bad.

Held items​

Each Astral can hold one item, which works by itself in every battle. Give one from the Astrals page or the Stash Tin in the Fanny Pack. The held items are listed in Items and Shops.

Winning: Vibes, levels and moves​

Beat an Astral and your team earns Vibes (experience). The Vibes are shared between the Astrals that were sent out against it and are still standing. Trainers' Astrals give twice as many as wild ones, and the higher you are, the more you earn (see Intoxication).

With enough Vibes an Astral peaks: it goes up a level, its stats grow a little (by luck, see Astrals and Types), and now and then it learns a new move.

An Astral knows up to four moves. When it wants to learn a fifth, a page shows the new move's numbers and, under the cursor, what each move does. Pick the move to forget, or Skip the new one. The game asks once more before you give a move up. Nothing is lost for good: Juniper the tarot reader can bring forgotten or skipped moves back later (see Juniper's Tarot).

Beaten wild Astrals sometimes leave something behind: usually water or coconut water, sometimes a melon slice or a sachet, rarely Tiger Balm. Trainers pay you in rupees instead.

Trainers​

  • A trainer brings one to six Astrals and sends the next one in when one fades out.
  • You cannot bag a trainer's Astral ("You cannot bag somebody else's trip.") and you cannot run.
  • Under the Shift battle style (the default), when you knock out one of their Astrals you are told who comes next and may Swap Astral for free before it arrives, or Keep going. See Options.
  • Wraparound Shades in your bag make trainers notice you from one tile less far away.

When your own Astral fades out, you must choose who goes next.

Bosses​

The eight wristband bosses, and a few other big names, get the full treatment:

  1. An entrance. The music drops, the crowd turns and the camera slides over to them.
  2. A cutscene introducing them, with their own title card.
  3. Their own stage. Each boss fights on a set of their own, to their own theme music.
  4. The moment. The first time a boss's team falls to half its total Serotonin, the boss says one line and changes the fight: the music speeds up and their Astral gets faster, the type chart flips, everyone gets higher, they heal, or their team toughens up. Be ready for it.
  5. The ceremony. Win, and the crowd cheers while you receive your wristband and learn what it lets you do (see Wristbands and Techniques).

Lose, and you white out (see Chai Tents and Whiteouts). When you come back for another go, the cutscene plays again in full.

The tent and shops next to a boss's stage stay shut until that boss is beaten. Come prepared.

Rematches​

Every wristband boss eventually offers a stronger rematch, about ten levels up, with the same moment at half health. It becomes available once you have collected a couple more wristbands after beating them. DJ Diabolo only takes his during his headline set at the Poi Pit, and he will tell you when to come back. The Promoter's waits until after the credits. Rumour has it that beating all eight rematches wakes something under the Mainstage.

Losing​

When your last Astral fades out, you do not black out. You white out. You lose some rupees and possibly an outfit, and wake up hours later at the last tent where you rested. The details are in Chai Tents and Whiteouts.

At the very top of your high, you may meet your own reflection: a battle against a copy of your own party. Losing to it costs nothing at all. Winning gives nothing either. It is a reflection.
