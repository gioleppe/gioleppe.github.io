Intoxication

"Out here everything gets you a little high: every fight, every snack, the air." — Prof. Banyan

How high you are is the game's big dial of risk and reward. Higher means more Vibes, better catches and rarer Astrals. It also means harder wild fights, clumsy hands and costlier whiteouts. How high to fly is your call.

The four bands​

There are four levels of high. Once you are past a Microdose, the band shows in the corner of the screen, and the Fanny Pack always shows a meter with your band under it.

Critical hits also get more common the higher a battle is, for both sides.

A fight also has its own level of high: it starts at yours, nudged up by the Astrals in it (some of them are trippier than others), and moves with certain moves and boss moments. For Vibes, bagging and fumbles, what counts is the band you were in when the battle started.

When you enter a new band, a line tells you so:

  • "You are coming up. Wild Astrals feel closer, and every fight teaches a little more."
  • "You are peaking. Rare Astrals come out to look at you. So do the strong ones. Your hands are getting unreliable."
  • "Ego gone. Everything is an Astral and every Astral is enormous. Drink some water, or do not."

Going up​

  • The Glowing Blotter. It is in your fanny pack from the very start, and it never runs out, "which is the problem". Each lick takes you up about one band: three licks from stone sober takes you all the way to Ego gone. Lick it with L, the LICK button on a phone, Y on a gamepad, from Quick Items, or from the Stash Tin. Not in the middle of a battle.
  • Boredom. Stand still outdoors for about 25 seconds with the blotter in your bag, and your hand finds it by itself. "Everybody does. Keep moving." Boredom never takes you past Ego gone, and nobody licks a blotter out of boredom in somebody's living room.
  • Battles. Every fight leaves you a little higher than before, more so after a trippy one.
  • Mystery Powder, some moves, and a couple of bosses' moments.

Coming down​

  • Walking. Every step brings you down a tiny bit. A long walk sobers you up slowly.
  • A Water Bottle. Giving one to an Astral means you take a sip too: about one band down. It always works, even when the Astral is not thirsty.
  • A rest. Chai, watermelon and a heavy blanket at any Chai Tent (or a bed) brings you all the way down to a Microdose.
  • A whiteout also leaves you stone sober, among other things.

What being high changes​

The rules above are only half of it. The festival itself looks different.

  • From the Come-up: the picture starts to ripple and the colours get richer. Glowing things get a soft halo, and the music picks up a warm pad underneath. Encounter tiles shimmer where a high-only Astral is waiting.
  • Peaking: the screen sways in waves to the beat, and mandalas fade in on the ground near you. About fifty people at the festival suddenly have something different to say. Some people only show up when you are Peaking, and some quests only move forward in this state.
  • Ego gone: the world wobbles and doubles, rainbows trail behind you, and text starts to scramble. You will see things that are not there (or are they?). Some places only exist while your ego is gone, and the moment you come down they fold shut and put you somewhere else. On the beach and the trail you might run into your own reflection, who wants a battle with your own team.

High-only Astrals​

Some Astrals simply do not exist for sober people. A few appear from Peaking, the rarest only at Ego gone, and some of those only at night or on the water. Each place where one hides has somebody who drops a hint until you have seen it. The map and the Journal mark those places with a little eye.

Is it safe to be high?​

In the game, yes, mostly. Nothing is permanent: rest at a tent and you are back to a Microdose. The real risks are practical:

  • wild Astrals come more often and hit harder,
  • you waste bags on fumbles,
  • and a whiteout costs you up to twice as much money.

A good rhythm for many players: get high to train and hunt rare Astrals near a tent, then rest before a boss.

tip

If the wobbling screen is too much, turn on Grounded mode in Options. The rules of being high still apply, but the picture stays calm.
